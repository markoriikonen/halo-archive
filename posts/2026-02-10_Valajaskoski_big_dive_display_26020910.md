### 2/10 - 2026-02-10 16:21 UTC

9/10 Feb 2026, Rovaniemi. 19 x 13s, the very best stage of the second plate intensification swarm, and which I didn't blunder by overexposing. My reason to concentrate on this was to get more data on the 58° zenith circle. It seems to have no outright red visible, only blue, therefore being solely a blue edge effect from snow reflection, an "integrated countesun" (explanation suggested by Lefaudeux), no arc from 90° prism refraction involved.

No big lights in the neighborhood, the only lights are pretty much what you see to the right of the image, quite far away on a level ground and well shaded against upwards light, giving only the meagrest pillars, if at all. So this display was solely caused by the double parking lot flood. Very intense – like daytime underneath.

Another blue edge effect is visible on the parhelic circle outer edge. Zenith arc is abnormally close to the light because of the high elevation. And of course there are the distinct 120° parhelia curving below the light level. They look reddish, so are we talking about the common red rim effect here?

The spot at the zenith bothers me a bit. Zenith spot should be present, but am I seeing color in it?

The third image is b-r, fourth g-r.
