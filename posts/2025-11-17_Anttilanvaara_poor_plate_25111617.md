### 3/8 - 2025-11-23 11:59 UTC

16/17 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. An earlier stage to the above stack, 17 x15s. I thought of seeing Moila from the side of the beam. Looks like there is indeed some V-shape.

But also something below the lamp that I did not see. That smudge (denoted by arrow) must be lower 9° plate arc. Not the usual kind from raypath 3-26, but the other one from 23-6 raypath because of the lamp elevation. That opens the game for the flip 9° plate arcs grouped around flipsun, but I didn't see any sign of them no matter how I adjusted the stack. Maybe it is an artefact. I mean, there is no corresponding 24° plate arcs.

What comes to seeing Moila from the side, you always perceive halos better from outside of the beam. In the middle everything kind of just glitters disturbingly. Camera, however is not bothered by this, I always try to have it in the middle of the beam. If the throw is really long, then it is more ok to admire the display in the middle.

### 4/8 - 2025-11-24 08:19 UTC

16/17 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. Another stack 50 minutes earlier to the above stack allowed a better look at the near-lamp area because of darker exposure. I am now more confident of the 9° lower 23-6. Also 9° halo and no question about Moila. But still don't see the lower 24°.

Now, of the four 9° plate arcs, the other upper arc, 3-16, is still up for grabs. I feel like it could be present, but Moila disturbs. The international race for capturing it is still on.

This stack is only 8 x 15s. Then I increased the exposure. The lesson learned: the inner area is more important, keep it dark. This will also help with the possible flip 9° plate arcs grouped around flipsun.

### 5/8 - 2025-11-24 08:26 UTC

16/17 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. Three simulations to study the 9° plate arcs and flip 9° plate arcs. A thin upper pyramid (second simulation) is not enough to produce the upper 9° 3-16. Tilting weakens the flip pyramid plate arcs much more than the non-flip arcs. 2 degree tilts were used, which is an optimistic value, I think usually the tilts are more.

### 6/8 - 2025-11-24 08:47 UTC

16/17 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. I opt for the camera being exactly in the center of the beam. It is a precision job, the more so the shorter the throw. If you are adjusting the lamp, it is a question of millimeters, if you adjust the tripod, it is a question of moving it centimeters. Here I realized the beam was shooting a bit over the camera and walked down to adjust the lamp. Looks to me like the second image would have been centered good, but I settled for the third.
