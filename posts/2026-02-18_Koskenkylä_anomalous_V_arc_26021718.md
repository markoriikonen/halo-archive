### 7/21 - 2026-02-21 16:18 UTC

17/18 Feb 2026, Rovaniemi. The vertical extent of the feature bothers me. So here is one more idea based on crossed crystals. Crossed plates are common in snow guns displays and if they were crossed at a certain range of angles, an abnormally broad arc would ensue. Here I have imitated crossings at 64-68°.

TapeMoilanen tells that they have not observed certain kind of polycrystals forming discreet angles, but a type of triple polycrystal would form discreet angles and thus could make new halos (pages 183-184).

In the literature the crossing angle of ~70° is often referred,  such as in "Structures and Formation Mechanisms of Snow Polycrystals" by Furukawa, figure 8 of that 1982 paper depicting such crossed plate. Weinheimer and Knight brought up crystals joined at this angle in 1987 paper "Scheiner's halo: Cubic ice or polycrystalline hexagonal ice?". Interestingly, in figure 1 of that paper there is a table by Lee of measured crossings of actual crystals, showing that there is a range of angles. The original by Lee, "On the Crystallographic Orientation of Spatial Branches in Natural Polycrystalline Snow Crystals", has a couple of nice photos of crossed plates.
