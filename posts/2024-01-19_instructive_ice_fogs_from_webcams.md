### 1/3 - 2024-01-19 19:56 UTC

1/3. Shots from the two Rovaniemi panorama cameras on 17 January are instructive on the variety of ice fog origins. In the Ounasvaara camera video here (which includes also the next day) we see from 1120 a parhelion that looks formed in the receding edge of low cloud / fog. How it came about? I thought of what might be regarded as natural nucleation that sometimes happens at the edges of dissipating low clouds but no. The post 2/3 from the Koivusaari cam shows that the cloud and so the display was from snow guns.

The same video also demonstrates what happens when they turn off the pressure air. Say bye bye to the ice fog as the huge combined plume from each snow gun dwarfs to the puniest of individual exhalations. Depending on whether you look at railway station or Apukka data, the temperature started falling at 7 or 4 am. The worker at the ski center had told me earlier that they turn off the air at around -25 C. Well this switch-off very likely happened at a bit warmer as the temp at the railway station, which is representative of the slopes better than the cold spot Apukka, was -20 C.

But what about the outbreak of ice fog in this post's video at 1350 when the whole scene becomes foggy and parhelia reappears? Looking opposite to the sun reveals the airport as the source, see post 3/3. Most like a plane had taken off giving birth to a growing cloud that briefly engulfed a part of the city.

Amid all of this the tiny detail of Suosiola power plant plume descending to the ground in the distance and continuing to do so the whole of the 18th (the arrow) might seem insignificant. But that's where I photographed halos on both 17/18 and 18/19 nights (a closer-up of the plume early evening on 17/18: https://t.co/NxOYsG2piD). It was cold, down to -40 C.

The fog over the river near Jätkänkynttilä bridge is just that – water fog from a persistent opening in the ice cover.
