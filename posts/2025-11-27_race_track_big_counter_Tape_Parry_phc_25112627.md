### 6/20 - 2025-11-30 11:25 UTC

26/27 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. And here is a simulation testing the idea that the slight intensity increases on 46° halo could be flip parhelia (arrows), allowing for the possibility of the the upper flip parhelic circle being plate instead of Parry made (the second image is the stack with those intensifications). Well, the message simulation conveys is that even if those were flip parhelia, they are so faint that no flip parhelic circle is produced.

Moreover, the brightenings seem to be not on the flipsun level, but closer to horizon. I think the brightenings are, as I have stated above, just part of the lens aftefact ring,and the so the flip parhelic circle is solely Parry made.

If that is accepted by the community, then we have unalloyed cases of all the three flip parhelic circles photographed: plate, Lowitz and Parry.  For plate and Parry there are the upper and lower versions (the Parry lower flip parhelic circle still to be captured). The Lowitz flip parhelic circle has no upper and lower differentiation because, due to the spinning of the crystals, it is the same raypaths that are making it, no matter whether the light is above or below horizon. It's the same thing as with tanarc – no upper and lower arc, just one arc that has 2 contact points.

Limited rotation changes the calculus, however. Then the raypaths are not the same, meaning upper Lowitz flip parhelic circle and lower Lowitz flip parhelic circle could be differentiated. As we know, Lowitz crystals are, according to halo simulations, almost always wobbling rather than spinning, and the same is at least sometimes true of column orientation. I don't know how this should be dealt with. Thus far I have been preferring go with with the theoretical full orientation and so no upper / lower separation.

### 20/20 - 2026-04-20 06:48 UTC

26/27 Nov 2025 Rovaniemi. Here is an account of the night. It was written before I had looked at the photos.

Alarm goes off at 9 pm after about one hour of eye shut. That will take me through the night, if need be. In an meanwhile FMI has updated its next hours forecast from partially clear to fog. As I am hauling myself and equipment to the car, fog starts to enshroud the scene. I feel the excitement rising: temp at railway station -9 C.

At racetrack it's becoming more foggy, too. Still a bit windy but already turning into diamond dust. I ponder where to set up the camp. At the far end of the racetrack a flimsy wooden stand allows to do extreme negative elevations, but it is rather exposed to the headlights of youth driving the main road back and forth. There is the whole Fury Road of them – cars, buggies, motorcycles and some doomsday lightbars on the roof. So I drive inside the fenced racetrack area, place the Olight Javelot Turbo at the bottom of the pit, camera up on top of a snow mound. Not fully insulated from the passing cars' lights, but much better than at the stand.

What comes to diamond dust, I soon see that my choice of the exact spot is not optimal. After a little action with not so well oriented crystals, it becomes almost solely fog, with only occasional transformations to diamond dust, whereas some 150 meteres to the left the two stadium building lights are giving a display. The spot where I am has a slightly back-angle view to those lights and I see only parhelic circle sticking out of them, but that's enough to understand that something of massive proportions must be going on there. To save seconds I take the car to go to inspect Even if these are just floodlights, the display is insane. I ponder what to do. I could move the camp to the other side of the stadium building for a hopefully better ice fog environment. It's risky, the change could mark a permanent shift to the west, but I decide to do nothing, half ouf of laziness. A good move, for once. Not that many minutes later the super-swarm shifts again and comes where we are waiting.

The best diamond dust transformation burts are pure crystalline ultraviolence. But then it craps again. This time I see the core of the action has moved to the opposite direction to my left; only 20 meters or so to the left crystals are glittering more constantly. I can see this when I point my Acebeam that way. I hesitate, but it is getting only worse, becoming constant water fog. I recall some cases where the display stayed hours in one exact spot. Like once in Himos with Jari Luomanen, where moving 100 meters to any direction on the lake ice was fatal. The streelight Bottlinger in Kangasala (likewise with Luomanen) was also stationary, small scale affair. Horror images of waiting in this spot all night for the diamond dust to shift eventually compel to pack up and move the camp 20 meters to the left.

Better covered now with action, the display is still not that impressive. Tanarc is not well defined, which I put down to still too windy conditions. But then the material quality start improving with diffuse arc and flip-helic arc entering the scene in gushs of crystal transformation. I pay attention also to the supralateral arc extension reaching the zenith. It's my second time to have visual of this halo.

I see no parhelia / flip-parhelia whatsover. And this sets my agenda for the night: photograph a purebreed Parry upper flip-parhelic circle. At the outset, it doesn't augur good: I don't see any indication of Parry orientation, no eponymous shallowcave arc against the ground, no helic arc. And I can't have consolation from Robert Greenler saying that Parry orientation is subset of column orientation. Yes, stopping the spinning of column oriented crystals so that a prism face is horizontal gives you Parry crystals, but the reality of the actual crystal clouds do not agree well with this subset take. In diamond dust displays Parry orientation halos almost always occur in displays with plate halos. True, these displays always have at least a little bit of column, but interestingly, column displays without plate halos are almost always without Parry (I know only one exception).

So, at the outset it looks a forlorn hope for the Parry crystals to enter this swarm. But without trying you won't know, so I set my chin to photograph straight up because, of all displays, this is the one to have a try. The lamp is some -18 degree elevation and the zenith view covers the both the upper flipsun and flip-anthelic point. If Parry enters the scene, its upper flip-parhelic circle will appear, just like flip-Liljequist, at the back hemisphere near the flip-anthelic point, while at the same time lacking of flip-parhelia on the front hemisphere would proof that plate orientation was absent and so plate upper flip-parhelic circle is not an option for explanation for the stripes.

Parry flip-parhelic circle hunt aside, it is of course true that straight up view is the default, but this time its role was risen to higher prominence by the fog. Even during the most violent crystal transformations fog never disappeared completely, the massive glow on the lamp side of which rendered photographing front hemisphere pretty useless (took only some example shots). And talking of fogbow(s), which was ever-present feature, in the past my experience has been that halos and fogbows tend be exclusive of each other, i.e. that fog and diamond dust do not have tendency to be mixed. Although I have photographed displays which proof otherwise, they have been short-live affairs – I have never experienced such hours long swarm as now, where fog and fogbow is constant even during the highest crystal transformations.

But even if fog was ever-present this time, it was always in proportion to the diamond dust. The more intense the halo display, the weaker the fogbows and vise versa. I wouldn't have minded of those fogbows not messing up the view, but then again, wihtout that thick fog those bursts of crystal transformation would not likely have reached such ravaging levels of brutality (the likes of which I have only experienced before in the 7/8 Dec 2008 display in Rovaniemi).

All forecasts, FMI, Meteoblue and Yr predict increasing winds and warming starting 3-4 am. Even though there may thus be only one to two hours left of the action, as a safety caution I drive to the apartment to put in the charger the other one of the two Canon batteries I have, because it was starting to run on empty after heavy use. It is not one time that the forecasted wind speed increase due to an arriving front has been significantly delayed. I leave my Nikon at the site continuing to take photos in the Olight beam, it is only 5 minute drive to the apartment. It's not like

Upon coming back Nikon had stopped photographing. It has problems with interval timer, the "infinite" setting is actually very much finite. However, possibly nothing was missed as the last shots were of pure fog and it was fog when I arrived (didn't check the time on arrival to see how much was missed).

In addition to the flip upper Parry parhelic circle, another new halo I had in mind was the solar side counterpart of the Greenler arc. It is an extreme elevation feature, so possible from the wooden stand. I wonder how much fogbow might hamper its visibility, but then convince myself that those extreme crystals transformations would hands down win over the fogbow. As diamond dust doesn't seem to be coming back to the current spot, I pack up and drive to the wooden stand to give the Greenler arc non-basalf face reflection counterpart the old college try.

But this time it is TLHS: a huge tangent arc greets me from a single floodlight down at the horsetrack as I curve to the location and then, at the drop of a hat, the action is gone. Just fog. All the same, as fortune favours the prepared I set up the equipment, lamp on the ground, camera on tripod at the top the stand. But now even the fog disappears. Moreover, the twigs of bushes and tree saplings start to wave; wind is increasing. I am about to wrap it up when fog engulfs the scene again. But I see nowhere pillars. The gunning is not able to transform anymore this fog into diamond dust anywhere, an effect from the wind. Wind just increases and capturing the Greenler arc counterpart is left for next adventures. I pack up and leave for good, back at the apartment 3:40 am.
