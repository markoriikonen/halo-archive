### 1/77 - 2026-03-02 15:28 UTC

2 March 2026, Rovaniemi. The Juujärvi racing snowgun turned stratus into diamond dust in small area. Zenith arc must have been the best I have seen – for a short moment it shone in almost blinding brightness (was not taking photos at that particular moment, however).

Towards the end, when the air was already drying up, plate gave room for column and Parry.

I almost run out of memory card space. I have no time to deal with that massive material now, so I bought three 64 gb cards in case there is action tonight.

### 10/77 - 2026-03-04 15:43 UTC

2 March 2026, Rovaniemi. Two fringes: those of Martikainen and that of Zhang! 20 successive frames stacked, aligned manually. Cropped, original fisheye.

I had just packed up, ready to return to the open field and then saw the fringes of Martikainen. No time to put the camera back on the tripod (I have the painstaking, ancient screw attachment) so I resorted to the ol' emergency measure of placing it on any surface – which this time was the car roof.

Zhang's fringe inside the parhelion was a bonus feature – I did not pay attention to it this time. Most likely it was visual; things were just too busy.

I should say that I am not suggesting calling the other fringes after Martikainen. If that is to happen, it must be an honor to come from outside; the observer's countrymen can't do it. I am sure some technical name can be come up with once we understand the formation of this effect. Could this be just the same effect as the common red rim, only the colors are better sorted out because the exit apertures are of highly uniform size?

### 32/77 - 2026-03-12 14:33 UTC

2 March 2026, Rovaniemi. Simulations offer three options to explain the arc in the above post. Two types of Lowitz arcs, one of which is tangent to vex Parry arc, the other to shallowcave Parry; and Liljequist arc that is tangent to shallowcave Parry (this is usually called the "concave" arc as opposed to the "convex" arc seen in the earlier stages of this display and which theoretically is expected to be tangent to deepcave Parry arc). The gif flashes each of these arcs which are labelled in the next post.

My gut simply says that Lowitz arcs are not an option here. That leaves only the Liljequist arc, but there is a line of evidence about the observed cases being not exactly like the simulated arc. They are less inclined and not actually tangent to shallowcave Parry arc. This issue has been discussed in the past. See Jia Hao's comment and the link to my post he provides it the Halo Vault post https://t.co/MIXywLbvNE ; and also Xiao Yifei's last comment and my reply with a link in a Taivaanvahti post: https://t.co/4DGqm7mivK

If the arc is not tangent to shallowcave Parry, it probably prevents calling it the "shallowcave" type of Liljequist arc. Better just to stick with the old concave, and convex for the deepcave. But even these are problematic because of an existence of another convex arc that doesn't cross counterparhelion, but shoots past it on the outside (e.g: https://t.co/w4MZsmHixT). That also places the usage of "Liljequist" in the name in precarious position because we can't know whether the arc he observed was of the crossing or non-crossing type.

It appears, then, there is certain wisdom in Tape's "parhelion flares", "parhelion legs" and "parhelion arms". They leave open both the theory and history. They are not really names, but descriptions of what is seen in the most common of observing situations. Theoretically the arcs are not related to parhelia but to counterparhelia.

A suggestion that I got from Tape in an email, "subparhelic arcs", takes this into account. To avoid the problems that come with "sub" (and "super"), in my language it would transform to "counterparhelic arcs" or "flip-parhelic arcs".

What comes to the history part, although there is no knowing whether Liljequist's convex arc was of the crossing or non-crossing type, I think if his name is used in the first place (in my mind it is OK to have parallel technical and observer names, for example), it should be reserved for the crossing type for the reason that he attempted several pages of calculations of the crossing arcs. (I say "attempted" because Tape once wrote in an email that he did not understand Liljequist's calculations).

Well, I could continue to drone on about this and that angle to the issue, but that would get me only tangled further in the endless vines of the halo nomenclature jungle. Better to move on.
