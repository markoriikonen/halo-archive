13/14 Dec 2025, Rovaniemi. A display in Anttilanvaara. I wonder about the faint feature marked by arrows. Integrated flip-sun blue edge from snow reflection? If so, then it is curious that it appears on the rear-hemisphere where the ground was not lit.

Another possibility would be (counter)zenith arc from low situated surrounding lights, but all lights are far away and in the direction opposite to the lamp there was nothing. For reference of this type thing, see the lunar display on the night of 13/14 December 2013 in Koli, Finland, that included both the counterzenith arc and its Dufay from the ski center floods:

### 5/5 - 2026-04-19 09:35 UTC

13/14 Dec 2025, Rovaniemi. Earlier on, I had written a pdf to desktop drawer of this display. This is what I wrote about the next stage:

Then I tested if the integrated divergent flipsun blue edge can be summoned in a positive-light set-up, camera on the ground so the snow around is brightly lit by the hot spot (10 x 30s). No success. The only thing I can associate with the lit snow is the zenith spot, conforming to the elongated shape of the lit area.

But the display was already in a decline, so I think this needs to be replicated in a stronger display, and preferably in a clear-sky one. And also take photos so that the lamp is right under the tripod, pointing down.

Indeed, later in February I happened on a stronger display which contained the integrated flipsun blue edge and also managed to pull off the lamp-right-under-the tripod experiment, as recounted in this thread: https://t.co/fPLWOHVUgo

I am not sure, though, if the spot at the zenith (in both displays) should be regarded as zenith spot or just countersun from the elongated lit patch on the ground. Maybe the distinction is unnecessary. Far as I can tell, this is not zenith spot from direct lamp light. In the February display the spot at the zenith is extending a tail in the direction opposite of the lamp https://t.co/3whCohStCK  If it was formed directly from the lamp photons, the tail would be towards the lamp.

In the original pdf there is some more info, like simulation attempts on those parhelion related arcs: https://t.co/7IHs7s58E3
My take on some things have changed in the intervening time, so not everything is in line in the pdf with what I have written in this thread.
