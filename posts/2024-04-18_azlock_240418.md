### 1/2 - 2024-04-18 15:24 UTC

The puddle ice tends to shatter as you work to get your piece, but this morning (18 April 2024, Ylikylä snow deposit, Rovaniemi) I got lucky by being able to extract a big plate. Interestingly, the Ujvarosi star was better when I turned the inner side facing the sun (I still want to confirm the orientation in coming-up sessions). This time the parhelia on each spike were the major attraction. When I measured the original Ujvarosi star by Ujvarosi some years ago, the parhelia spots turned out to be a little further out than 22° halo. Actually, they were at the same distance as Parry arc at zenith sun is.

A cutting tool, angle grinder, might help to cut gently and get off bigger pieces. Gotta have it for the next season.

These crystals are not easy to photograph and at night leading to this morning my attempts were unsuccessful. In the daytime I got some poor crystal photos.

### 2/2 - 2024-04-18 15:24 UTC

I was wrong about the "antisolar Ujvarosi star". There is no such animal. It is subsun point and those are the same arcs that you see when you have the plate horizontally under the sun. This video (18 April 2024, Ylikylä snow deposit, Rovaniemi) shows how I turn away from the sun and then back, all the while angling the plate to keep the arcs visible continuously.
