### 1/1 - 2026-04-06 11:28 UTC

The other set suggests the ring is some kind of an artefact (by the camera?), although I would have preferred the set cover more fully the zenith region.

In case it would happen be turn out to be a real halo by some future observation, any surfaces providing parallel light reflection are out of question here. It was still full winter, everything snow covered.

As to the diffused light from sloping snow surface, to me it seems this is no way make an a halo at the location of that ring. Only the IZA, 58 degrees from the horizon, can form by diffused light coming from near horizon area because light from a range of near-horizon elevations gets accumulated at it. 

Is the diffuse snow reflection making anything at all in sun light? Counter-IZA is handicapped by the weakening basal face reflection in comparison to IZA. How about the integrated countersun (ICS) from snow reflection that is seen so well in streetlight displays, e.g. here: https://t.co/fPLWOHVUgo This is strong raypath in comparison to counter-IZA. Should it be able to form also in sun light? Could some blue of the Rovaniemi solar display IZA be actually from it? It's worth noting that in the lower lamp elevation view of the streetlight display https://t.co/0OkPd3LFOr the ICS actually seems to have some red color and quite clear Dufay-like enhancement at the lateral locations. I don't know what to think about all this.
