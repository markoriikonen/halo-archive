### 8/28 - 2026-03-18 15:15 UTC

2/3 March 2026, Rovaniemi. Parameters of the previous post's simulation. This is one of those Parry displays that are caused by crystals grown along the triangular dimension. Everything about Parry orientation halos in the display calls for using such crystals, the mean shape of which is displayed in the parameter table.

At this light elevation triangularish Parry crystals open the door for discovery of two novel halos. They are shown in the simulation that has only Parry and column orientation for clarity (I have touched upon these also in my nomenclature article).

One is an arc in the Ounasvaara family, from raypath 146. It is marked in the simulation by the lower arrow and may be called a type of infralateral Ounasvaara arc. Possibly it would have been visible were it not for the restless forest background.
 
The other one is a type of "rotated counter-Parry arc",  from raypath 346, denoted by the upper arrow. Not even the slightest suggestion of it is seen. One might try, but I doubt stacking all 17 frames would coax it out.

Triangulars are needed for the usual Ounasvaara arc, but it gets weak at this high light elevation. It's located at the helic arc – supralatarc crossing and some intensity enhancement is visible in that location in b-r, so possibly it is present. Taking it into account in simulation would seem to call for using even more triangular Parry crystals, up to the point where there is practically no shallowcave Parry at all, in which case the arc must be mainly Lowitz arc.

### 24/28 - 2026-03-21 07:08 UTC

2/3 March 2026, Rovaniemi. Why nobody told me that? Contrary to what I wrote in one of the earlier posts, it is actually possible to adjust the relative brightness between the retrograde raypath Lowitz arc pair (A and B / upper and lower). Just have the orientation equilibrium at a tilted angle.

Everything kind falls in place with this. Not only is the display's retrograde Lowitz arc situation reproduced right by the simulation, but also the Lowitz C / circular that seems to be present only above parhelia. This suggests there is an aspect ratio range where the triangulars take Parry orientation and then some range where they are "indecisive", wobbling in Lowitz orientation.
